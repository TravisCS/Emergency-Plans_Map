    About this map

    How to use it

    • Nodes. Each node is one organization: a city, a county department, a state or federal agency, a utility, a campus or a neighborhood group. Click it for its documents. Each document links to the official copy; when the official link is dead, an Internet Archive copy is linked too. Opened from the folder on the building Mac, each document also links to the local PDF.
    • Find my plans. Type an address, click the map, or pick a city. The page finds the city or unincorporated area that contains the point (LA County city boundaries, point-in-polygon in the browser), then lists the city's plans, the City of Los Angeles annexes when inside Los Angeles, the council of governments that includes the city, Los Angeles County's plans, state and federal frameworks, and nearby neighborhood guides, campuses and airports within a few kilometers. Special districts and county-wide partners are listed for reference: whether a water or air district serves an address depends on the provider.
    • Layers. Untick a level to hide its nodes. Browse searches organizations and document titles.
    • Links. The address bar of the page does not change; use "Copy link" on a card to share a node, or the link under a lookup result to share a point.

    Where the documents come from

    The library behind this map was built in October 2026 from the official websites of the organizations themselves: each city's emergency operations plan, local hazard mitigation plan and general-plan safety element, Los Angeles County's operational area plans, the State of California's emergency plan and annexes, mutual-aid agreements, federal frameworks and law, after-action reports, hazard maps and the plans of airports, utilities, campuses and neighborhoods. Research agents found the links, a download script fetched them, each file was checked to be a PDF, and a second pass read the first pages of every file and compared them with the catalogue (). The map reads the same record as the workbooks and reference lists in the library folder.

    Link audit

    How the nodes were placed

    Each organization was placed at its headquarters, city hall or site by two research agents working from the organization's name and documents, with a precision tag (hq, site or city) and a confidence tag. Positions mark the organization that issued a document, not the area a plan covers: a county plan covers the whole county, a state plan the whole state. Documents of state and federal agencies sit at their headquarters (Sacramento, Mather, Washington, Reston), so zoom out to see them.

    Sources

      Tools and method

      Leaflet 1.9.4 (BSD-2-Clause), one HTML page with its data in two JavaScript files, no server. Coordinates WGS 84 (EPSG:4326), drawn in Web Mercator (EPSG:3857). City boundaries were simplified with a Douglas-Peucker pass (about 25 m tolerance) from the county's polygons. Address search uses the OpenStreetMap Nominatim service when the page is opened from a folder or a website; inside a claude.ai artifact outside requests are blocked, so click the map or pick a city there. Code and layout generated with Claude (Anthropic); data, placement and accuracy reviewed by Travis Cody Smith. Research agents (Claude) found the links and checked the files; a Claude agent audited each link by byte-range request, and links that refused scripts were checked again in a browser.

      Limitations

      • Node positions for about one organization in eight are marked medium confidence and may be off by a few hundred meters; the About card of each node shows its tag.
      • Council-of-governments membership and the Palos Verdes Peninsula grouping come from the councils' own membership lists as of 2026; special district service areas are not mapped.
      • The library has the documents that were public in October 2026; a city may have adopted a newer plan since. Twenty documents could not be downloaded by script and are listed as "manual" with their official link only.
      • Unincorporated areas are shown as one class; community names (Altadena, Topanga, Acton) come from the documents, not from a community boundary layer.
